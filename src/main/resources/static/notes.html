Problem on Trains

Introduction

Problems on trains involve calculating the time taken by a train to pass a stationary object like a pole, platform, or another train. The key concept is understanding relative speed and how the length of the train affects the time taken to cross objects of different lengths.

Speed Conversion Formulas

km/hr to m/s: Speed (m/s) = Speed (km/hr) × (5/18)

m/s to km/hr: Speed (km/hr) = Speed (m/s) × (18/5)

This conversion is essential because train problems often require consistent units. The factor 5/18 comes from dividing 1000 meters by 3600 seconds, which equals 5/18.

Key Formulas

  • Time to pass a pole/stationary object: Time = Length of Train / Speed
  • Time to pass a platform: Time = (Length of Train + Length of Platform) / Speed
  • Time to pass another train (same direction): Time = (Length1 + Length2) / (Speed1 - Speed2)
  • Time to pass another train (opposite direction): Time = (Length1 + Length2) / (Speed1 + Speed2)
  • Relative speed (same direction): Difference of speeds
  • Relative speed (opposite direction): Sum of speeds

Important Concepts

  • Formation of Train: When a train passes a point, it travels a distance equal to its own length.
  • Platform Crossing: The train must travel its own length plus the platform length to completely cross it.
  • Two Trains: When two trains cross each other, the total distance covered is the sum of both lengths.
  • Direction Matters: Same direction uses difference of speeds, opposite direction uses sum of speeds.

Solved Examples

Example 1: Train Passing a Pole

A train 150 meters long is running at a speed of 60 km/hr. How long will it take to pass a pole?

Step 1: Convert speed to m/s
60 km/hr = 60 × (5/18) = 50/3 = 16.67 m/s

Step 2: Apply formula
Time = Length / Speed = 150 / (50/3) = 150 × 3/50 = 9 seconds

Answer: 9 seconds

Example 2: Train Passing a Platform

A train 200 meters long passes a platform 300 meters long in 25 seconds. Find the speed of the train in km/hr.

Step 1: Total distance to cover = Train length + Platform length
Distance = 200 + 300 = 500 meters

Step 2: Calculate speed in m/s
Speed = Distance / Time = 500 / 25 = 20 m/s

Step 3: Convert to km/hr
Speed = 20 × (18/5) = 72 km/hr

Answer: 72 km/hr

Example 3: Two Trains Passing Each Other

Two trains 150m and 200m long are running on parallel tracks in opposite directions at 45 km/hr and 55 km/hr respectively. How long will they take to completely pass each other?

Step 1: Calculate relative speed (opposite directions)
Relative Speed = 45 + 55 = 100 km/hr

Step 2: Convert to m/s
100 × (5/18) = 500/18 = 250/9 m/s

Step 3: Total distance = Sum of lengths
Distance = 150 + 200 = 350 meters

Step 4: Calculate time
Time = 350 / (250/9) = 350 × 9 / 250 = 12.6 seconds

Answer: 12.6 seconds

Practice Tips

  • Always convert units before calculations to ensure consistency
  • Remember that when passing a stationary object, only the train's length matters
  • When passing a platform or bridge, add both lengths
  • For two trains, always add their lengths to get total distance
  • Draw a diagram if the problem seems complex to visualize the scenario

Time and Work

Introduction

Time and work problems deal with calculating how long it takes to complete a job when one or more people or machines work together. The fundamental principle is that work done is proportional to the time spent and the efficiency of the worker.

Basic Formulas

Work Done: Work = Rate × Time

Rate of Work: Rate = Work / Time

Time taken: Time = Work / Rate

Complete work = 1 unit

Work done by A in 1 day: 1/A (where A = days to complete work alone)

Combined Work Formula

Two people A and B working together:
1/T = 1/A + 1/B

Time taken together: T = (A × B) / (A + B)

Three people A, B, and C:
1/T = 1/A + 1/B + 1/C

Efficiency and Work Relationship

  • If A is x times efficient as B: Efficiency ratio A:B = x:1
  • Time ratio: Time taken by A : Time taken by B = 1:x
  • Work done is inversely proportional to time taken
  • If A takes x days, A's daily work = 1/x of total work
  • If A and B work for n days: Work done = n × (1/A + 1/B)

Solved Examples

Example 1: Two Workers Together

A can do a piece of work in 10 days and B can do it in 15 days. How long will they take to complete the work if they work together?

Step 1: Calculate individual work rates
A's work per day = 1/10
B's work per day = 1/15

Step 2: Add the rates
Combined work per day = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6

Step 3: Calculate time
Time = 1 / (1/6) = 6 days

Answer: 6 days

Example 2: Using the Direct Formula

A takes 8 hours and B takes 12 hours to complete a job. How long will they take working together?

Using formula T = (A × B) / (A + B):
T = (8 × 12) / (8 + 12) = 96 / 20 = 4.8 hours

Answer: 4.8 hours (or 4 hours 48 minutes)

Example 3: Three Workers

A, B, and C can complete a work in 20, 30, and 60 days respectively. How long will they take working together?

Step 1: Calculate individual rates
A: 1/20, B: 1/30, C: 1/60 per day

Step 2: Add the rates
1/20 + 1/30 + 1/60 = 3/60 + 2/60 + 1/60 = 6/60 = 1/10

Step 3: Calculate time
Time = 1 / (1/10) = 10 days

Answer: 10 days

Example 4: Work Done by One Person After Another

A and B together can complete a work in 12 days. A alone can complete it in 20 days. How long will B alone take to complete the work?

Step 1: Let B take x days alone
Combined work = 1/20 + 1/x = 1/12

Step 2: Solve for x
1/x = 1/12 - 1/20 = 5/60 - 3/60 = 2/60 = 1/30

Step 3: x = 30 days

Answer: B takes 30 days alone

Practice Tips

  • Always assume total work = 1 unit for easier calculations
  • Work done is additive, so add individual work rates when people work together
  • Remember that efficiency and time are inversely related
  • For pipes and tanks (filling/emptying), apply the same principles with positive and negative rates

Profit and Loss

Introduction

Profit and loss problems deal with buying and selling of goods. The key is understanding the relationship between cost price, selling price, profit or loss, and their respective percentages. These concepts are fundamental in business mathematics.

Basic Terms and Formulas

Cost Price (CP): Price at which an item is bought

Selling Price (SP): Price at which an item is sold

Profit = SP - CP (when SP > CP)

Loss = CP - SP (when CP > SP)

Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100

Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100

Finding Selling Price

SP with x% Profit: SP = CP × (1 + x/100)

SP with x% Loss: SP = CP × (1 - x/100)

SP when profit% and discount% given:
First apply discount, then calculate profit on reduced price

Finding Cost Price

CP when SP and x% profit given: CP = SP / (1 + x/100)

CP when SP and x% loss given: CP = SP / (1 - x/100)

Special Cases

  • Successive Profit/Loss: When profit% and loss% are applied successively, use the formula for successive percentage changes
  • Faulty Weights: Gain% = [(Error)/(True Value - Error)] × 100
  • Discount: SP = MP × (1 - Discount%) where MP is marked price
  • When two equal profit% and loss%: Always results in loss

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Profit Calculation

An article is bought for Rs. 500 and sold for Rs. 650. Find the profit percentage.

Step 1: Calculate profit
Profit = SP - CP = 650 - 500 = Rs. 150

Step 2: Calculate profit percentage
Profit% = (150 / 500) × 100 = 30%

Answer: 30% profit

Example 2: Finding Selling Price

A shopkeeper buys an article for Rs. 1200 and wants to make a profit of 25%. What should be the selling price?

Using formula:
SP = CP × (1 + Profit%/100)
SP = 1200 × (1 + 25/100) = 1200 × 1.25 = Rs. 1500

Answer: Selling price = Rs. 1500

Example 3: Loss Calculation

A man sells a table for Rs. 2760 and loses 8%. What was the cost price?

Using formula:
CP = SP / (1 - Loss%/100)
CP = 2760 / (1 - 8/100) = 2760 / 0.92 = Rs. 3000

Answer: Cost price = Rs. 3000

Example 4: Successive Discounts

A shirt is marked at Rs. 2000. First a discount of 20% is given, then another discount of 10% on the reduced price. Find the final selling price.

Method 1: Step by step
After 20% discount: 2000 × (1 - 20/100) = 2000 × 0.80 = Rs. 1600
After 10% discount: 1600 × (1 - 10/100) = 1600 × 0.90 = Rs. 1440

Method 2: Single equivalent discount
Equivalent discount = 20 + 10 - (20×10)/100 = 28%
SP = 2000 × (1 - 28/100) = 2000 × 0.72 = Rs. 1440

Answer: Final selling price = Rs. 1440

Practice Tips

  • Always calculate profit/loss based on cost price, not selling price
  • For successive percentage changes, apply each change sequentially to avoid errors
  • When finding CP from SP with profit%, divide SP by (1 + profit/100)
  • Remember that selling price must be greater than cost price for profit, and vice versa

Problems on Ages

Introduction

Problems on ages involve finding current ages, past ages, or future ages of people based on given relationships and conditions. The key principle is that the age difference between any two people remains constant over time.

Key Concepts

  • Age difference: Always remains constant regardless of time
  • Present age: Let present age = x years
  • Age after n years: Present age + n
  • Age n years ago: Present age - n
  • Ratio given: Use ratio to set up equations
  • Product of ages: Sometimes given, useful for quadratic equations

Basic Formulas

If current ages are A and B:
Ratio A:B = m:n

Age after k years: A+k and B+k (ratio may change)

Age k years ago: A-k and B-k

When ratio changes:
Set up equation: (A + k)/(B + k) = new ratio

Important Observations

  • The ratio of ages changes with time, but the difference remains constant
  • If two persons' ages are in ratio m:n, then their ages can be expressed as mx and nx
  • The present age is always the best starting point for variables
  • When the ratio is given at a specific time (past or future), adjust accordingly

Solved Examples

Example 1: Current Ratio Given

The ratio of ages of A and B is 3:5. If A is 12 years old, how old is B?

Using ratio:
A:B = 3:5
A = 3x = 12, so x = 4
B = 5x = 5 × 4 = 20 years

Answer: B is 20 years old

Example 2: Future Ratio Given

Father is three times as old as his son. After 5 years, he will be two and a half times as old as his son. Find their present ages.

Step 1: Let son's present age = x years
Father's present age = 3x years

Step 2: Set up equation for future
(3x + 5) = 2.5 × (x + 5)

Step 3: Solve the equation
3x + 5 = 2.5x + 12.5
3x - 2.5x = 12.5 - 5
0.5x = 7.5
x = 15

Step 4: Find both ages
Son = 15 years, Father = 3 × 15 = 45 years

Answer: Son is 15, Father is 45

Example 3: Past Ratio Given

Ten years ago, P was half as old as Q. If P is now 20 years old, how old is Q now?

Step 1: Current age of P = 20 years
Ten years ago, P was 20 - 10 = 10 years old

Step 2: Use the ratio
10 = (1/2) × Q's age 10 years ago
Q's age 10 years ago = 20 years

Step 3: Find current age
Q's current age = 20 + 10 = 30 years

Answer: Q is 30 years old

Example 4: Age Difference Application

The sum of ages of a mother and daughter is 50 years. Five years ago, the mother was four times as old as the daughter. Find their present ages.

Step 1: Let daughter's present age = x years
Mother's present age = (50 - x) years

Step 2: Set up equation for 5 years ago
(50 - x - 5) = 4 × (x - 5)
(45 - x) = 4x - 20

Step 3: Solve
45 + 20 = 4x + x
65 = 5x
x = 13

Step 4: Find both ages
Daughter = 13 years, Mother = 50 - 13 = 37 years

Answer: Daughter is 13, Mother is 37

Practice Tips

  • Always express all ages in terms of one variable (preferably the present)
  • Remember that age difference remains constant over time
  • When setting up equations, ensure both sides are from the same time point (all present, all past, or all future)
  • Use the ratio to express multiple ages when a relationship is given

Average

Introduction

Average is a measure of central tendency that represents the typical value in a dataset. It is calculated by summing all values and dividing by the number of values. Averages are widely used in statistics, academics, and daily life for comparison and analysis.

Basic Formulas

Arithmetic Mean (Average):
Average = (Sum of all values) / (Number of values)

Weighted Average:
Weighted Avg = Σ(weight × value) / Σ(weights)

Using Deviation:
Average = Assumed Avg + (Σdeviation) / n

Average Speed Formulas

Average Speed (different distances):
Avg Speed = Total Distance / Total Time

NOT Average of Speeds:
Avg Speed ≠ (Speed1 + Speed2) / 2

Same distance at different speeds:
Avg Speed = 2 × S1 × S2 / (S1 + S2)

Same time at different speeds:
Avg Speed = (S1 + S2) / 2

Effect of Adding/Removing Values

  • New sum: Old sum ± new value
  • New average after adding: (Old sum + new value) / (n + 1)
  • New average after removing: (Old sum - removed value) / (n - 1)
  • If all values equal: Average = that value
  • Deviation method: Useful when values are large or have a pattern

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Average

Find the average of numbers: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50.

Method 1: Direct formula
Sum = 10 + 20 + 30 + 40 + 50 = 150
Number of values = 5
Average = 150 / 5 = 30

Method 2: Using assumed average
Assume average = 30
Deviations: -20, -10, 0, 10, 20
Sum of deviations = 0
Average = 30 + 0/5 = 30

Answer: 30

Example 2: Weighted Average

A student scores 80 in Math (weight 3), 75 in Science (weight 2), and 90 in English (weight 1). Find the weighted average.

Calculate weighted sum:
(80 × 3) + (75 × 2) + (90 × 1) = 240 + 150 + 90 = 480

Total weight:
3 + 2 + 1 = 6

Weighted Average:
480 / 6 = 80

Answer: 80

Example 3: Average Speed

A car travels 100 km at 50 km/hr and returns the same distance at 25 km/hr. Find the average speed for the entire journey.

Note: Average speed ≠ (50 + 25)/2 = 37.5 km/hr

Total Distance: 100 + 100 = 200 km

Time for first part: 100 / 50 = 2 hours
Time for return: 100 / 25 = 4 hours

Total Time: 2 + 4 = 6 hours

Average Speed: 200 / 6 = 33.33 km/hr

Using Formula: 2 × 50 × 25 / (50 + 25) = 2500 / 75 = 33.33 km/hr

Answer: 33.33 km/hr

Example 4: Finding Missing Number

The average of 6 numbers is 25. If one number is excluded, the average of the remaining 5 numbers is 20. Find the excluded number.

Total sum of 6 numbers: 6 × 25 = 150

Sum of remaining 5 numbers: 5 × 20 = 100

Excluded number: 150 - 100 = 50

Answer: 50

Practice Tips

  • Never average the averages directly - always use total sum divided by total count
  • For average speed, always use total distance divided by total time
  • The deviation method is faster for large numbers with a common base
  • When values are in arithmetic progression, average = (first + last)/2

Permutation and Combination

Introduction

Permutation and combination are counting techniques used to calculate the number of ways to arrange or select objects. Permutation considers order (arrangements), while combination does not (selections). These concepts are fundamental in probability and combinatorics.

Key Definitions

Factorial (n!): n! = n × (n-1) × (n-2) × ... × 2 × 1

0! = 1 (by definition)

Permutation (nPr): Number of arrangements of r objects from n where ORDER MATTERS

Combination (nCr): Number of selections of r objects from n where ORDER DOES NOT MATTER

Formulas

Permutation:
nPr = n! / (n-r)!

Combination:
nCr = n! / (r! × (n-r)!)

Relationship:
nPr = nCr × r!

Note: nCr = nC(n-r) (complementary selection)

Special Cases

  • Circular Permutation: (n-1)! for arranging n distinct objects in a circle
  • Linear arrangement: n! for all n objects in a line
  • Repetition allowed: n^r for arrangements with repetition
  • Selection with repetition: C(n+r-1, r)
  • Arrangement with restrictions: Subtract invalid cases from total
  • nC0 = nCn = 1

When to Use Which

  • Permutation: Arranging people in rows, letters in words, ranks, positions
  • Combination: Selecting committee members, drawing cards, choosing items
  • Ask yourself: Does order matter in this arrangement?
  • Example: AB and BA are different (Permutation) but same (Combination)

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Permutation

How many different ways can 3 books be arranged from 5 different books?

Using formula:
nPr = n! / (n-r)!
5P3 = 5! / (5-3)! = 120 / 2! = 120 / 2 = 60

Step by step:
First book: 5 choices
Second book: 4 choices
Third book: 3 choices
Total = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60

Answer: 60 ways

Example 2: Basic Combination

How many ways can a committee of 3 be chosen from 5 people?

Using formula:
nCr = n! / (r! × (n-r)!)
5C3 = 5! / (3! × 2!) = 120 / (6 × 2) = 120 / 12 = 10

Using complementary:
5C3 = 5C(5-3) = 5C2 = 10

Answer: 10 ways

Example 3: Circular Permutation

In how many ways can 4 people be seated around a round table?

For circular arrangements:
(n-1)! = (4-1)! = 3! = 6

Explanation:
In a circle, rotations of the same arrangement are considered identical. Fix one person's position and arrange the others.

Answer: 6 ways

Example 4: Word Arrangement

How many different words can be formed using the letters of "MATHEMATICS"?

Step 1: Count total letters
Total = 11 letters

Step 2: Count repeated letters
M appears 2 times, A appears 2 times, T appears 2 times

Step 3: Apply formula
Words = 11! / (2! × 2! × 2!) = 39916800 / 8 = 4989600

Answer: 4,989,600 different words

Example 5: Mixed Problem

From a deck of 52 cards, how many ways can we select 1 ace and 1 king?

Selecting ace: 4C1 = 4 ways

Selecting king: 4C1 = 4 ways

Total ways: 4 × 4 = 16 ways

Answer: 16 ways

Practice Tips

  • Ask "Does order matter?" to decide between permutation and combination
  • For repeated objects, divide by the factorial of each repetition count
  • Use nCr = nC(n-r) to simplify calculations when numbers are large
  • For circular arrangements, always use (n-1)!

Time and Distance

Introduction

Time and distance problems deal with calculating speed, time, or distance when two of these quantities are known. These problems frequently appear in aptitude tests and require understanding the fundamental relationship between these three variables.

Basic Formulas

Speed: Speed = Distance / Time

Distance: Distance = Speed × Time

Time: Time = Distance / Speed

Units must be consistent (km/hr, m/s, etc.)

Unit Conversion

km/hr to m/s: Multiply by 5/18

m/s to km/hr: Multiply by 18/5

km/hr to m/min: Multiply by 1000/60 = 50/3

m/s to m/min: Multiply by 60

Average Speed Formulas

General Formula:
Avg Speed = Total Distance / Total Time

Same distance at different speeds S1 and S2:
Avg Speed = 2 × S1 × S2 / (S1 + S2)

Same time at different speeds:
Avg Speed = (S1 + S2) / 2

Note: Arithmetic mean only works when times are equal!

Relative Speed

  • Same direction: Relative speed = S1 - S2 (difference)
  • Opposite direction: Relative speed = S1 + S2 (sum)
  • Time to meet (opposite): Distance / (S1 + S2)
  • Time to catch up (same): Distance / (S1 - S2)

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Calculation

A car travels at 60 km/hr for 3 hours. How far does it travel?

Using formula:
Distance = Speed × Time
Distance = 60 × 3 = 180 km

Answer: 180 km

Example 2: Finding Time

A train covers a distance of 240 km at a speed of 80 km/hr. How long does it take?

Using formula:
Time = Distance / Speed
Time = 240 / 80 = 3 hours

Answer: 3 hours

Example 3: Unit Conversion

A boy runs 100 meters in 10 seconds. Find his speed in km/hr.

Step 1: Speed in m/s
100 / 10 = 10 m/s

Step 2: Convert to km/hr
10 × (18/5) = 36 km/hr

Answer: 36 km/hr

Example 4: Average Speed

A person travels from A to B at 40 km/hr and returns at 60 km/hr. If the distance is 120 km each way, find the average speed for the whole journey.

Method 1: Using formula
Avg Speed = 2 × 40 × 60 / (40 + 60) = 4800 / 100 = 48 km/hr

Method 2: Total distance/Total time
Total Distance = 240 km
Time going = 120/40 = 3 hours
Time returning = 120/60 = 2 hours
Total Time = 5 hours
Avg Speed = 240 / 5 = 48 km/hr

Answer: 48 km/hr

Example 5: Relative Speed

Two cars start from the same point and move in opposite directions at 50 km/hr and 60 km/hr. How far apart will they be after 2 hours?

Relative speed (opposite directions):
50 + 60 = 110 km/hr

Distance after 2 hours:
110 × 2 = 220 km

Answer: 220 km apart

Practice Tips

  • Always ensure units are consistent before calculations
  • Use the harmonic mean formula for average speed when distances are equal
  • Draw a diagram for complex relative speed problems
  • Convert km/hr to m/s by multiplying by 5/18 for easier mental calculations

Simple Interest

Introduction

Simple interest is a method of calculating interest where interest is charged only on the original principal amount. It is widely used in short-term loans, certificates of deposit, and certain savings accounts. The interest remains constant throughout the tenure.

Basic Formulas

Simple Interest (SI):
SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Where:
P = Principal (original amount)
R = Rate of interest per year (%)
T = Time in years

Amount (A):
A = P + SI = P + (P × R × T)/100
A = P × (1 + (R × T)/100)

Finding Individual Components

Find Principal:
P = (SI × 100) / (R × T)

Find Rate:
R = (SI × 100) / (P × T)

Find Time:
T = (SI × 100) / (P × R)

Find Amount:
A = P + (P × R × T) / 100

Time Conversions

  • Years to months: T(years) = T(months) / 12
  • Months to years: T(years) = T(months) / 12
  • Days to years: T(years) = T(days) / 365 (or 360 in some cases)
  • Quarterly rate: R/4 per quarter
  • Half-yearly rate: R/2 per half-year

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Simple Interest

Find the simple interest on Rs. 5000 at 6% per annum for 2 years.

Using formula:
SI = (P × R × T) / 100
SI = (5000 × 6 × 2) / 100 = 60000 / 100 = Rs. 600

Answer: Rs. 600

Example 2: Finding Amount

A sum of Rs. 2000 is invested at 5% per annum simple interest. What amount will be received after 3 years?

Calculate SI:
SI = (2000 × 5 × 3) / 100 = 30000 / 100 = Rs. 300

Calculate Amount:
A = P + SI = 2000 + 300 = Rs. 2300

Answer: Rs. 2300

Example 3: Finding Principal

A sum of money amounts to Rs. 6500 at 8% per annum simple interest in 2 years. Find the principal.

Using formula:
A = P × (1 + (R × T)/100)
6500 = P × (1 + (8 × 2)/100) = P × (1 + 0.16) = P × 1.16

Solve for P:
P = 6500 / 1.16 = Rs. 5603.45 (approximately)

Answer: Rs. 5603.45

Example 4: Finding Rate

At what rate per annum will Rs. 2000 become Rs. 2600 in 5 years at simple interest?

Calculate SI:
SI = A - P = 2600 - 2000 = Rs. 600

Using formula:
R = (SI × 100) / (P × T) = (600 × 100) / (2000 × 5) = 60000 / 10000 = 6%

Answer: 6% per annum

Example 5: Finding Time

In how many years will Rs. 1500 become Rs. 2100 at 10% per annum simple interest?

Calculate SI:
SI = 2100 - 1500 = Rs. 600

Using formula:
T = (SI × 100) / (P × R) = (600 × 100) / (1500 × 10) = 60000 / 15000 = 4 years

Answer: 4 years

Practice Tips

  • Always ensure time is in years when using the basic SI formula
  • Simple interest is linear - interest is the same every year
  • For fractional years, convert to decimal (e.g., 6 months = 0.5 years)
  • Memorize the formulas for finding each component (P, R, T, A)

Percentage

Introduction

Percentage means "per hundred" and is a way to express a number as a fraction of 100. It is used to compare quantities, calculate changes, and express proportions. The symbol % represents "per hundred" or division by 100.

Basic Formulas

Percentage:
Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

Find X% of Y:
(X/100) × Y

Find what % X is of Y:
(X/Y) × 100

Fraction to Percentage:
Multiply by 100

Percentage Change

Percentage Increase:
((New - Original) / Original) × 100

Percentage Decrease:
((Original - New) / Original) × 100

Net Change:
Final - Initial (can be positive or negative)

Successive Percentage Changes

For x% increase then y% increase:
Net change = x + y + (x × y) / 100

General formula for successive changes:
Effective % = A + B + (A × B) / 100 (for increases)
Effective % = A - B - (A × B) / 100 (for opposite changes)

Multiplier method:
For x% change, multiply by (1 ± x/100)

Quick Conversion Tricks

  • 10% = 1/10, 20% = 1/5, 25% = 1/4
  • 50% = 1/2, 75% = 3/4, 33.33% ≈ 1/3
  • To find 5%, find 10% and divide by 2
  • To find 15%, add 10% and 5%
  • To find 20%, double 10%

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Percentage

What percentage is 25 of 80?

Using formula:
Percentage = (25 / 80) × 100 = 0.3125 × 100 = 31.25%

Answer: 31.25%

Example 2: Finding Part from Percentage

If 30% of a number is 150, what is the number?

Let the number be x:
30% of x = 150
(30/100) × x = 150
0.3x = 150
x = 150 / 0.3 = 500

Answer: 500

Example 3: Percentage Increase

The price of a shirt increased from Rs. 400 to Rs. 500. What is the percentage increase?

Using formula:
Increase = 500 - 400 = 100
Percentage Increase = (100 / 400) × 100 = 25%

Answer: 25%

Example 4: Successive Percentage

The population of a town increases by 10% each year. If the current population is 1,00,000, what will it be after 2 years?

Method 1: Step by step
After 1st year: 100000 × 1.10 = 110000
After 2nd year: 110000 × 1.10 = 121000

Method 2: Single calculation
Effective increase = 10 + 10 + (10×10)/100 = 21%
Population = 100000 × 1.21 = 121000

Answer: 1,21,000

Example 5: Successive Decrease

A shop gives successive discounts of 20% and 10%. What is the single equivalent discount?

Using formula:
Effective discount = 20 + 10 - (20×10)/100 = 30 - 2 = 28%

Method with multipliers:
Original price = 100
After 20% discount: 100 × 0.80 = 80
After 10% discount: 80 × 0.90 = 72
Total discount = 100 - 72 = 28%

Answer: 28%

Practice Tips

  • Memorize common fraction-percentage conversions for faster calculations
  • For successive changes, always apply changes sequentially to avoid errors
  • When calculating percentage increase/decrease, always use original value as denominator
  • Use multiplier method (1 ± r/100) for successive percentage calculations

Ratio and Proportion

Introduction

Ratio is a comparison of two quantities by division, showing how many times one quantity contains or is contained within the other. Proportion is an equation that states two ratios are equal. These concepts are essential in solving various aptitude problems.

Basic Definitions

Ratio:
a:b = a/b (read as "a is to b")

Proportion:
a:b = c:d (read as "a is to b as c is to d")

Product of means = Product of extremes:
If a:b = c:d, then b × c = a × d

Continued proportion:
a:b = b:c, so b² = a × c

Types of Proportions

Mean Proportional:
Between a and b: √(a × b)

Third Proportional:
If a:b = b:c, then c = b²/a

Fourth Proportional:
If a:b = c:d, then d = (b × c)/a

Compound Ratio:
(a:b) × (c:d) = (a×c):(b×d)

Componendo and Dividendo

If a/b = c/d, then:

Componendo:
(a + b)/b = (c + d)/d

Dividendo:
(a - b)/b = (c - d)/d

Componendo-Dividendo:
(a + b)/(a - b) = (c + d)/(c - d)

Division in Given Ratio

  • To divide X in ratio a:b:
    Part 1 = (a/(a+b)) × X, Part 2 = (b/(a+b)) × X
  • For ratio a:b:c:
    Parts = (a/(a+b+c)) × X, etc.
  • Ratios can be simplified by dividing by GCD
  • Direct proportion: As one increases, other increases
  • Inverse proportion: As one increases, other decreases

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Ratio

Divide Rs. 500 in the ratio 3:2.

Total parts: 3 + 2 = 5 parts

Value of one part: 500 / 5 = Rs. 100

First share: 3 × 100 = Rs. 300
Second share: 2 × 100 = Rs. 200

Answer: Rs. 300 and Rs. 200

Example 2: Finding Fourth Proportional

Find the fourth proportional to 3, 5, and 15.

Using formula:
If a:b = c:d, then d = (b × c)/a
d = (5 × 15) / 3 = 75 / 3 = 25

Answer: 25

Example 3: Mean Proportional

Find the mean proportional between 4 and 16.

Using formula:
Mean proportional = √(4 × 16) = √64 = 8

Answer: 8

Example 4: Componendo-Dividendo

If (x + y)/(x - y) = 5/3, find x/y.

Using Componendo-Dividendo:
If (x + y)/(x - y) = 5/3

Then x/y = (5 + 3)/(5 - 3) = 8/2 = 4

Answer: x/y = 4

Example 5: Age Ratio Problem

The ratio of ages of A and B is 4:5. After 3 years, the ratio becomes 5:6. Find their present ages.

Let present ages be 4x and 5x:

After 3 years:
(4x + 3) / (5x + 3) = 5/6

Cross multiply:
6(4x + 3) = 5(5x + 3)
24x + 18 = 25x + 15
18 - 15 = 25x - 24x
3 = x

Present ages:
A = 4 × 3 = 12 years
B = 5 × 3 = 15 years

Answer: A is 12, B is 15

Practice Tips

  • Always simplify ratios by dividing by GCD
  • When dividing a quantity, find total parts first, then divide
  • For proportion problems, use cross-multiplication to avoid confusion
  • Componendo-Dividendo is useful when sums and differences are involved

Probability

Introduction

Probability measures the likelihood that an event will occur. It is expressed as a number between 0 and 1, where 0 indicates impossibility and 1 indicates certainty. Probability is fundamental in statistics, gambling, weather forecasting, and many scientific fields.

Basic Formulas

Probability of an event E:
P(E) = Number of favorable outcomes / Total number of possible outcomes

Probability range: 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

Sum of probabilities: P(E) + P(E') = 1 (where E' is complement)

Odds in favor: P(E) : P(E')

Odds against: P(E') : P(E)

Types of Events

Independent Events:
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

Mutually Exclusive Events:
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)

Non-Mutually Exclusive:
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)

Conditional Probability:
P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B)

Important Concepts

  • Certain Event: P(E) = 1
  • Impossible Event: P(E) = 0
  • Complementary Event: P(not E) = 1 - P(E)
  • Equally Likely Outcomes: Each outcome has same probability
  • Addition Rule: For OR events
  • Multiplication Rule: For AND events

Card, Dice, and Coin Basics

  • Standard deck: 52 cards, 4 suits, 13 ranks each
  • Die: 6 faces (1-6)
  • Coin: 2 outcomes (H, T)
  • Red card probability: 26/52 = 1/2
  • Prime number on die: 3/6 = 1/2 (2, 3, 5)
  • Even number on die: 3/6 = 1/2 (2, 4, 6)

Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic Probability

What is the probability of getting a head when tossing a fair coin?

Total outcomes: 2 (H, T)
Favorable outcomes: 1 (H)
Probability: P(H) = 1/2 = 0.5

Answer: 1/2 or 0.5

Example 2: Rolling a Die

Find the probability of getting an even number when rolling a fair die.

Total outcomes: 6
Even numbers: 2, 4, 6 = 3 outcomes
Probability: P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2

Answer: 1/2

Example 3: Drawing a Card

A card is drawn from a standard deck. Find the probability of drawing a king.

Total cards: 52
Number of kings: 4
Probability: P(King) = 4/52 = 1/13

Answer: 1/13

Example 4: Independent Events

A coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of getting two heads.

Method 1: Sample space
HH, HT, TH, TT (4 outcomes)
Favorable: HH (1 outcome)
P = 1/4

Method 2: Multiplication rule
P(HH) = P(H) × P(H) = (1/2) × (1/2) = 1/4

Answer: 1/4

Example 5: Mutually Exclusive Events

A die is rolled. Find the probability of getting a number 3 or 5.

Total outcomes: 6
Favorable for 3: 1 outcome
Favorable for 5: 1 outcome

Since mutually exclusive:
P(3 or 5) = P(3) + P(5) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3

Answer: 1/3

Example 6: Complement Rule

In a class of 50 students, 30 are boys. If a student is selected at random, what is the probability that the student is a girl?

Number of girls: 50 - 30 = 20
Probability: P(girl) = 20/50 = 2/5 = 0.4

Using complement:
P(girl) = 1 - P(boy) = 1 - 30/50 = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4

Answer: 2/5 or 0.4

Practice Tips

  • List all possible outcomes to avoid missing any in the sample space
  • Use multiplication for AND (independent events), addition for OR
  • Use complement rule when finding "at least one" or "not" probability
  • Remember that favorable outcomes must be counted carefully for cards and dice
